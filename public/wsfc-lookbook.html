Why one ink

The case for a monochrome range, for the committee.

ReasonWhat it means
It costs lessOne screen instead of four. Tees drop roughly $3–4 a unit at print, and both tee colourways share one origination charge.
Everything matchesBlack, white and grey go together on a boat, at the bar and at the gala. No colourway ages out; the range still works at the centenary.
The badge stays specialFull colour appears once: on the hero print and the gala collateral. Everywhere else the mark is quiet, so when the colour version turns up, it means something.
Safety colour keeps its meaningWith the range in mono, the only orange the club puts on a body is a life jacket. That is the point.

Illustrative pre-quote figures. Confirm with supplier. Moving to one-colour print lifts tee margin by roughly $4 a unit against the v1 four-colour spec; at the same ~$8,000 production order the range returns roughly $16–19k at sell-through. Pre-orders de-risk quantities before the main run.

Life jacket note: never manufacture. Brand onto approved PFDs only (Hutchwilco or RFD, NZS 5823 compliant), decorated to the manufacturer's spec so certification is not voided. Fund it through the Next 40 Years Fund or a sponsor; the lending-library units double as floating advertising.

Status programme note: the production run must include the Sixty Black cap and beanie (same 9FORTY A-Frame and beanie blanks as the standard range, only thread and blank colour change, so no extra origination). These are allocated, never sold at general retail: they are the visible reward of the Black · Gold · Silver programme, and the win-back offer ("re-join by 30 Sep, walk in as Black") depends on the cap existing.
